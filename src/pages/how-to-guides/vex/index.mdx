# Working with VEX in DevGuard

Every scan produces findings, and a large share of them do not actually affect your product. **VEX
(Vulnerability Exploitability eXchange)** is the standard for saying so in a way that machines — your own
dashboard, your auditors' tooling, your customers' scanners — can read. This section covers everything you
do with VEX in DevGuard: recording a decision, automating it, importing decisions from suppliers, and
publishing your own.

If you first want to understand the standard itself rather than the workflow, read
[What is a VEX Report](/explanations/vulnerability-management/what-is-vex).

## Pick the right tool

DevGuard offers two ways to state something about a vulnerability, and the difference is reach.

A **vulnerability event** — `Accept` or `False Positive` — assesses one specific finding, once. It is the right
choice when the reasoning genuinely applies to this finding alone. See
[Create Vulnerability Events](/how-to-guides/vulnerability-management/create-vuln-events).

A **VEX rule** pairs a CEL expression with the same decision and applies it to every finding that matches —
those already in the repository and any that later scans turn up. See
[Create & Manage VEX Rules](/how-to-guides/vex/vex-rules).

<Callout type="info">
  A vulnerability handled by a VEX rule is **locked** — you cannot change its state by hand while the rule
  exists. To reopen it, delete or edit the rule.
</Callout>

## The typical workflow

1. **Triage a finding.** Open a dependency vulnerability and look at the *Path to component* graph — it
   shows exactly how the vulnerable package enters your product.
2. **Decide whether it is exploitable.** Is the vulnerable code shipped, reached, and reachable by an
   attacker?
3. **Record the decision.** Click the graph edge that is not actually traversed; DevGuard pre-fills a VEX
   rule for you. Or write your own expression in the playground — see the
   [CEL Expression Reference](/how-to-guides/vex/cel-reference).
4. **Pick a justification.** A false positive requires one of five standardized reasons. See
   [Choose a Mechanical Justification](/how-to-guides/vex/mechanical-justifications).
5. **Publish it.** Your decisions become machine-readable CycloneDX VEX, OpenVEX and CSAF documents. See
   [Export & Publish VEX](/how-to-guides/vex/export-vex).

<Callout type="info">
  **VEX rules themselves are never published.** What you hand out are the VEX statements they produce — the
  decision on each finding, not the CEL expression that made it. To share them, enable public access to
  vulnerability data in **Repository Settings** and pass on the public VEX, OpenVEX or CSAF URL.
</Callout>


Symmetrically, your suppliers publish their own VEX documents — DevGuard consumes them and turns each
statement into a rule for your asset. See
[Import VEX from Files & Suppliers](/how-to-guides/vex/import-vex).

Beyond suppliers, DevGuard also pools rules **across DevGuard organizations**: if enough independent
organizations have already written the same rule for a vulnerability you're triaging, it appears as a
crowdsourced recommendation you can adopt with one click, weighted by an organization trust score so a single
account can't manufacture consensus. See
[Create & Manage VEX Rules](/how-to-guides/vex/vex-rules#from-a-crowdsourced-vex-recommendation).

## Where to find VEX in the web UI

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| VEX rules, playground, upstream sources, recommendations | **Dependency Risks → VEX Rules** (asset level) |
| Assessing a single finding, path graph, rule pre-fill | **Dependency Risks → \<a vulnerability\>** |
| Public SBOM / VEX / CSAF URLs, Paranoid Mode | **Repository Settings** |
